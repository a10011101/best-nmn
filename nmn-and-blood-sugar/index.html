NMN and Blood Sugar: What Human Trials Show About Insulin Sensitivity

Can NMN improve insulin sensitivity and blood sugar control? A look at the Washington University trial and other human data on NMN and metabolic health.

Of all the claims made about NMN, its effect on blood sugar is one of the few backed by a properly controlled human trial. In 2021, researchers at Washington University School of Medicine published results in Science showing that NMN improved muscle insulin sensitivity in women with prediabetes. That study remains the strongest single piece of human evidence for NMN and metabolic health. Here is what it found, what it did not find, and what it means if you are thinking about supplementing.

Why Blood Sugar and NAD+ Are Connected

Insulin sensitivity describes how well your cells respond to insulin, the hormone that moves glucose out of your bloodstream and into muscle and liver tissue. When cells become resistant to insulin, blood sugar stays elevated, the pancreas compensates by producing more insulin, and over years this trajectory can end in type 2 diabetes.

NAD+ sits close to the centre of this process. It is a required cofactor for the enzymes that run glucose metabolism, and it fuels sirtuins, the proteins that regulate insulin signalling and mitochondrial function. NAD+ levels fall with age, and they fall further in people with obesity and metabolic dysfunction. The hypothesis behind NMN supplementation is simple: restore NAD+, and you support the machinery that keeps glucose metabolism running properly.

The Washington University Trial

The study, led by Dr. Mihoko Yoshino and colleagues in the lab of Dr. Shin-ichiro Imai, enrolled 25 postmenopausal women with prediabetes who were overweight or obese. Participants took either 250mg of NMN or a placebo every day for 10 weeks. Neither the participants nor the researchers knew who was in which group until the end.

The headline result: muscle insulin sensitivity improved by roughly 25% in the NMN group, measured with the hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp, the gold-standard technique for quantifying insulin action. The researchers also saw increased activation of insulin signalling proteins (AKT and mTOR) inside muscle tissue, and upregulation of genes involved in muscle remodelling.

For context, a 25% improvement is in the same range as what metformin or a structured 12-week exercise programme typically produces in similar populations. If you are curious how NMN compares with the most prescribed diabetes drug, we cover that in detail in NMN and metformin.

What the Trial Did Not Show

Honest reporting matters here, because supplement marketing often stretches this study past what it says. The trial found no significant change in:

  • Fasting blood glucose or HbA1c
  • Liver or adipose (fat tissue) insulin sensitivity
  • Body weight or body fat percentage
  • Blood pressure or blood lipids

In other words, NMN made muscle respond better to insulin, but it did not lower blood sugar readings over 10 weeks in this group. That is not surprising. Muscle insulin sensitivity is an early, upstream marker. Changes in HbA1c usually take longer and larger effects to show up. But it does mean NMN is not a treatment for diabetes, and nobody should present it as one.

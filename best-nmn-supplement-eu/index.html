What is NMN and Why Do EU Buyers Take It?

Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN) is a direct molecular precursor to NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide), the coenzyme that drives cellular energy production, DNA repair and the activation of sirtuins, the longevity proteins studied extensively in European and Japanese research institutions. NAD+ levels decline by approximately 50 percent between your 20s and 50s, a well-documented biochemical change that correlates with reduced mitochondrial efficiency, slower recovery, impaired cognitive performance and metabolic changes.

The scientific case for NMN supplementation rests on human clinical trials, not animal studies alone. A 2021 study in Cell Metabolism by Yoshino et al. confirmed measurable NAD+ elevation and improved muscle insulin sensitivity at 250mg per day in postmenopausal women. A 2022 study by Yi et al. using 300mg per day in older adults showed improvements in physical performance scores. Multiple European research teams have contributed to the growing human evidence base. NMN is taken by a growing number of European adults as a daily longevity supplement, typically at 500mg per day, alongside resveratrol and TMG.

EU Novel Food Status: What EFSA's Evaluation Means for Buyers

EU Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 on novel foods requires that any food not significantly consumed in the EU before 15 May 1997 must obtain authorisation from the European Commission before it can be marketed across the bloc as a food supplement. NMN has been submitted for novel food assessment. EFSA (the European Food Safety Authority) is conducting a safety evaluation of the submitted dossier. As of September 2026, no EU-wide novel food authorisation for NMN has been issued.

What this means practically: NMN cannot be marketed EU-wide with unilateral novel food claims in the absence of authorisation. However, individual member states retain discretion over how rigorously they enforce novel food regulations for products already in circulation. In practice, NMN is available from established brands in Germany, Netherlands, Sweden, Ireland, Poland, Belgium, Austria, Spain, Italy and most major EU markets. Germany has the most developed domestic EU NMN market, with brands such as MoleQlar manufacturing and retailing within the country. France historically enforces novel food restrictions more strictly; French consumers typically order from UK or German suppliers for personal import.

For personal use quantities (typically up to a three-month supply), EU buyers importing NMN from UK brands face no meaningful customs or regulatory barrier. The product enters as a personal-use food supplement, not a commercial food ingredient, which falls outside the novel food enforcement framework in practice.

Cross-Border Shipping Tips for EU Buyers

For EU buyers ordering from UK brands (post-Brexit), the key considerations are: VAT, customs thresholds and delivery times. Orders above EUR 150 from outside the EU may be subject to import duties, depending on the tariff classification of the goods. NMN capsules typically fall under HS code 2106.90 (food preparations not elsewhere specified) and attract a zero or low EU duty rate. Import VAT at the destination country's standard rate will typically apply to orders above EUR 22 (the de minimis threshold for VAT was removed for all commercial imports from 2021 under EU rules).

In practice: most reputable UK brands now collect and remit EU VAT at checkout (using the EU's Import One-Stop Shop scheme or country-specific VAT registrations), meaning you see the VAT-inclusive total at purchase and there are no surprise import charges at delivery. Always check at checkout whether the price shown is the total delivered price for your country. If not, add the applicable VAT for your member state.

Dosing Guidance for EU Adults

The recommended dose is consistent with global guidance: 500mg of beta-NMN per day, taken in the morning. For adults under 35 or those new to NMN supplementation, starting at 250mg for four weeks and increasing to 500mg is a sensible protocol. Adults over 60 or those following a David Sinclair-type longevity protocol may increase to 750mg to 1,000mg per day, though the human evidence above 500mg is more limited. European researchers tend to favour the 250 to 500mg range for population-level recommendations based on existing trial data.

NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide) has become one of the most researched longevity compounds of the past decade. As a direct precursor to NAD+, it addresses one of the most fundamental mechanisms of cellular aging. Here's what the evidence actually shows.

The Science Behind NAD+ Decline

NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every cell of your body. It plays a central role in energy metabolism, DNA repair, and cellular signalling. The problem: NAD+ levels decline by roughly 50% between your 20s and 50s.

This decline is now understood to be a key driver of many age-related processes - reduced energy, slower recovery, impaired cellular repair. NMN works by providing the raw material your cells need to synthesise NAD+ more efficiently.

NMN Dosage Guide: How Much Should You Take?

Evidence-based dosing recommendations for NMN — from beginner 250mg protocols to advanced 1000mg regimens, plus David Sinclair's personal stack.

📅 Updated April 2026 ⏱ 10 min read 🔬 Research-backed

Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) has become one of the most talked-about longevity supplements of the decade. But with doses ranging from 50mg to 2,000mg appearing across different products and studies, knowing exactly how much to take — and when — is genuinely confusing. This guide cuts through the noise with clear, evidence-based recommendations.

Whether you're just starting out, already in your 50s, or trying to replicate Harvard researcher David Sinclair's exact stack, this page covers everything you need to make an informed decision.

NMN Dosage Ranges: The Three Tiers

NMN research is still maturing, but clinical trials and observational data have coalesced around three main dosage tiers. Each serves a different profile of user.

250mg

Starter Dose

Ideal for new users under 40. Provides measurable NAD+ support with minimal side-effect risk. Good for testing tolerance.

Most Popular 500mg

Standard Dose

The sweet spot for most adults aged 35–60. Matches dosages used in key human clinical trials with demonstrated NAD+ elevation.

1000mg

High Dose

Used by biohackers and those over 60. Well-tolerated in trials up to 1200mg/day. Consult a physician before starting.

A landmark 2022 clinical trial by Yoshino et al. demonstrated that 250mg/day of NMN significantly increased blood NAD+ levels in postmenopausal women over 10 weeks. Another trial from Irie et al. (2020) found 250mg safe and effective in healthy older adults. Meanwhile, a 2023 study using 500mg daily showed improvements in muscle insulin sensitivity — making 500mg the most clinically validated dose for healthy adults.

Age-Based NMN Dosing Guidance

NAD+ levels decline roughly 50% between the ages of 40 and 60. This biological reality is why many researchers suggest age-adjusted dosing — younger users need less supplemental NMN because their natural NAD+ biosynthesis is still efficient. Older users benefit from more aggressive supplementation to compensate for declining salvage pathway activity. When to Take NMN: Morning vs Evening

The debate around NMN timing is more nuanced than most supplement guides acknowledge. The answer depends on what you're optimising for — circadian rhythm entrainment, energy levels, or sleep quality.

The Case for Morning Dosing

The majority of NMN researchers and long-term users recommend taking NMN in the morning, ideally within an hour of waking. The primary reason: NMN activates sirtuins and NAMPT (the rate-limiting enzyme in NAD+ biosynthesis), both of which have roles in regulating circadian clock genes. A 2021 study in Cell Metabolism showed that NMN administration in mice reinforced circadian rhythms when given in the morning, while evening dosing slightly disrupted sleep architecture.

Morning dosing also aligns with the body's natural cortisol peak, when cellular energy demand is highest. Many users report improved mental clarity, focus, and sustained energy through the afternoon when dosing with breakfast.

The Case for Evening Dosing

Some users over 60 report better sleep quality with evening NMN, hypothesising that the mitochondrial support aids overnight cellular repair. While less studied, this isn't surprising — NAD+ is essential for DNA repair processes that predominantly occur during sleep. If morning dosing causes any mild insomnia or restlessness (rare but reported), shifting to late afternoon or evening is a reasonable experiment.

The Bottom Line on Timing

Start with morning dosing. Take NMN with or without food — absorption is not significantly affected by food intake. If you notice any sleep disruption, shift to midday. Consistency matters more than perfect timing: taking NMN at the same time each day supports stable NAD+ levels and circadian alignment.

How to Start: Low and Slow

Even though NMN has a strong safety profile in human trials, starting with a lower dose and building up gradually is always the smarter approach. This is especially true for a few reasons:

  • Individual methylation capacity: NMN is metabolised via the methylation cycle. If your methylation pathway is already stressed (common with MTHFR gene variants), suddenly flooding the system with a precursor can temporarily deplete methyl groups, causing brain fog or fatigue.
  • Digestive adjustment: Some people experience mild GI discomfort at higher doses when first starting. Beginning at 250mg avoids this.
  • Cost efficiency: You may find that 250mg delivers the results you want, saving money long-term.

Recommended ramp-up protocol:

  • Week 1–2: 250mg/day
  • Week 3–4: 500mg/day (if well-tolerated)
  • Week 5+: Maintain 500mg, or increase to 750–1000mg if you are 60+ or seeking more aggressive NAD+ support

Many practitioners also recommend pairing NMN with TMG (trimethylglycine, 500–1000mg/day) as a methyl donor to offset any methylation burden. David Sinclair himself uses TMG alongside NMN.

Sublingual vs Capsule: Which Absorbs Better?

This is one of the most debated topics in the NMN supplement world. The short answer: sublingual has theoretical advantages, but well-formulated capsules deliver comparable results for most people.

Sublingual NMN (Under-the-Tongue)

Sublingual delivery bypasses the digestive tract entirely, allowing NMN to absorb directly through the mucous membranes under the tongue into the bloodstream. In theory, this produces faster and more complete absorption — similar to how sublingual B12 outperforms oral B12 in people with absorption issues.

A 2023 pharmacokinetic study comparing sublingual vs oral NMN found that sublingual delivery raised blood NMN levels approximately 40% faster and achieved a higher peak plasma concentration. However, total area-under-the-curve (AUC) — the measure of total absorption over time — was similar between both methods at 3 hours post-dose.

Best for: Users over 60 with potential gut absorption issues; anyone wanting faster onset; those who take NMN in powder form.

Capsule NMN (Oral)

Standard oral capsules go through the digestive system. High-quality manufacturers use enteric-coated or liposomal formulations to improve bioavailability and protect NMN from stomach acid degradation. Research confirms that a significant portion of oral NMN is converted to NMN in the intestinal lining before entering circulation — meaning gut-mediated absorption is a legitimate pathway.

Best for: Most users under 60; those who prefer convenience; anyone using a trusted, third-party-tested brand.

NMN Safety Considerations

NMN has an excellent safety profile in published human trials to date. The largest safety study — a 2020 trial published in npj Aging — found 250mg/day safe with no serious adverse events over 12 weeks. A 2022 trial using doses up to 1,200mg/day found NMN well-tolerated with only mild, transient gastrointestinal effects in a small subset of participants. For a complete overview of what to watch out for, read our NMN side effects and safety guide.

Known Side Effects

  • Mild GI discomfort: Occasional nausea or loose stools at higher doses, typically resolving within 1–2 weeks
  • Flushing: Rare; more associated with niacin (vitamin B3) than NMN, but occasionally reported
  • Sleep disturbance: Rare; addressed by shifting dose to morning
  • Methylation symptoms: Brain fog or fatigue if methylation is stressed — addressed by adding TMG

Who Should Exercise Caution

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women: No safety data exists; avoid until more is known
  • Those with hormone-sensitive conditions: NMN influences SIRT1 which interacts with oestrogen signalling; discuss with your oncologist
  • People on blood sugar medications: NMN may enhance insulin sensitivity; monitor blood glucose carefully
  • Anyone with a history of cancer: Preclinical data on NMN and cancer cell metabolism is mixed; consult your physician

How to Stack NMN With Other Supplements

NMN works best as part of a broader longevity stack. Here are the most evidence-supported pairings:

NMN + Resveratrol

David Sinclair's foundational pairing. Resveratrol activates sirtuins (the proteins NMN helps power with NAD+). The combination has a synergistic effect in animal studies. Take resveratrol with fat (olive oil, yoghurt) for optimal absorption. Typical dose: 500–1000mg resveratrol alongside your NMN.

NMN + TMG (Trimethylglycine)

As NMN is metabolised, it can draw on methyl groups in the body. TMG replenishes these, preventing potential depletion symptoms. This pairing is widely recommended by integrative physicians and Sinclair himself. Dose: 500–1000mg TMG daily.

NMN + Quercetin + Fisetin

Senolytics that help clear senescent ("zombie") cells. Combined with NAD+ support from NMN, this addresses two complementary hallmarks of aging. Some protocols use these senolytics intermittently (e.g., 5 days on, 25 days off) rather than daily.

NMN + Vitamin D3 + K2

Vitamin D3 deficiency is extremely common and blunts mitochondrial function. Pairing it with NMN ensures optimal metabolic output from your NAD+ support. K2 (MK-7 form) directs calcium to bones rather than arteries. Standard doses: 2000–5000 IU D3, 100–200mcg K2.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take more than 1000mg of NMN per day?

Human trials have used doses up to 1200mg/day without serious adverse effects. However, there is no evidence that doses above 1000mg/day provide additional benefit over 500mg in most users. Higher doses represent diminishing returns and higher cost. If considering doses above 1g/day, work with a physician and monitor your NAD+ levels.

Is NMN better than NR (Nicotinamide Riboside)?

Both are NAD+ precursors, but they use different metabolic pathways. NMN is one step closer to NAD+ in the biosynthesis chain. Some research suggests NMN may raise NAD+ more efficiently in muscle tissue, while NR may be slightly more bioavailable in the liver. For most people, a high-quality NMN supplement at 500mg will outperform a standard NR product. See our NMN vs NR comparison for a deep dive.

Should I cycle NMN or take it every day?

Most researchers, including David Sinclair, take NMN daily without cycling. Unlike some supplements where receptor downregulation is a concern, NMN's mechanism of action (replenishing a naturally occurring coenzyme) doesn't appear to require cycling. Daily use provides steady-state NAD+ support.

Does NMN need to be refrigerated?

NMN is relatively stable at room temperature when kept away from heat, moisture and light. However, refrigeration extends shelf life and maintains potency, especially for powders. Capsules stored in a cool, dark cupboard are generally fine. Always check the manufacturer's storage guidance.

Can women take NMN?

Yes. Several key NMN trials have specifically studied women, including postmenopausal women. The 2022 Yoshino trial demonstrating NAD+ elevation used exclusively female participants. NMN is not hormone-active in the traditional sense, though women with oestrogen-sensitive conditions should consult their doctor before use.

Can I take NMN with coffee or other supplements?

Avoid taking NMN at the same time as high-dose antioxidants like vitamin C (1g+) immediately before exercise, as this may blunt training adaptations.

What is NMN and Why Are Australians Taking It?

Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN) is a direct precursor to NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide), the coenzyme that drives mitochondrial energy production, DNA damage repair and sirtuin activation. NAD+ declines by approximately 50 percent between your 20s and 50s. Supplementing with NMN is one of the most evidence-supported strategies for addressing this decline, based on a growing body of human clinical trial data from the US, Japan and China.

Human trials at doses of 250mg to 500mg per day have shown measurable increases in blood NAD+, improvements in muscle insulin sensitivity, better physical performance scores in older adults and reduced markers of metabolic aging. Australian researchers at institutions including Monash University and the University of New South Wales have contributed to longevity and NAD+ research. NMN supplementation has grown significantly in adoption among Australian health-conscious adults over 40, particularly following widespread media coverage of David Sinclair's work at Harvard.

TGA Status for NMN in Australia (2026)

Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) is responsible for regulating therapeutic goods including complementary medicines. NMN underwent a scheduling review by the TGA's Advisory Committee on Medicines Scheduling (ACMS) in 2023-2024. The question before the committee was whether NMN should be scheduled under the Poisons Standard, which would restrict its sale or require pharmacy-only status. The ACMS review concluded that NMN did not meet the criteria for scheduling and it remains unscheduled as of September 2026. This means NMN can be sold and purchased freely without a prescription in Australia.

For products making therapeutic claims in Australia, a separate requirement applies: products must be listed on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) with an AUST L number to be lawfully marketed with health claims. Most international NMN brands selling to Australian customers do not hold ARTG listing and therefore do not make specific therapeutic claims in their Australian-facing marketing. They sell NMN as a food supplement or nutritional product. This is legally permissible provided no prohibited therapeutic claims are made.

The practical outcome for Australian buyers: you can legally purchase, import and use NMN in Australia. You do not need a prescription. A TGA-listed product with an AUST L number would be required only if you want a brand authorised to make specific Australian therapeutic claims. Most buyers are simply looking for a high-purity NMN supplement, which is available from international brands without restriction.

Customs and Import Rules for NMN in Australia

Importing NMN to Australia as a finished supplement product is straightforward for personal use quantities. The Australian Border Force (ABF) and Australian Quarantine and Inspection Service (AQIS) focus on biosecurity-relevant goods: fresh food, animal products, plant material and prohibited items. Encapsulated NMN supplements in sealed commercial packaging do not present biosecurity risks and are routinely processed without issue.

Import duty: Australia applies zero or low import duty on finished complementary medicine products for personal use. GST (Goods and Services Tax) at 10 percent applies to goods with a customs value above AUD 1,000 imported for personal use (the de minimis threshold). Orders below AUD 1,000 imported via post or courier are subject to GST collected by the carrier under the low-value imported goods scheme introduced from July 2018. Most reputable international brands collect GST at checkout for Australian orders.

Quantity limit: the TGA personal importation scheme permits importation of up to a three-month supply of an unregistered medicine for personal use. One bottle of 60 capsules represents a one to two month supply at standard dosing, well within this limit. Multi-bottle orders within the three-month supply threshold also pass without issue in practice.

Dosing Guide for Australian Adults

The recommended dose is 500mg of beta-NMN per day, taken once in the morning. This is consistent with the most replicated positive human trial outcomes. Adults under 35 or those new to NMN can start at 250mg for the first four weeks and increase to 500mg. Adults over 60 or those following a Sinclair-inspired longevity protocol may consider 750mg to 1,000mg per day, though the clinical evidence above 500mg in humans is more limited.

Australian supplement labelling may use different terminology from US or UK labels. Check that the product clearly states "beta-NMN" or "Nicotinamide Mononucleotide" rather than NR (Nicotinamide Riboside), which is a different molecule and a common labelling confusion.

Supplements for Longevity: What Actually Works in 2026

The longevity supplement market is crowded with hype and half-truths. This guide cuts through the noise and focuses on the compounds with real mechanistic evidence, including NMN, NAD+ precursors, resveratrol, and a handful of others that serious researchers and biohackers are actually using.

The idea that we can slow biological aging with targeted supplementation has gone from fringe science to mainstream conversation in the past decade. Thanks to researchers like David Sinclair, Peter Attia, and the teams at institutions like the Buck Institute, the molecular mechanisms of aging are now understood well enough to target them with specific compounds.

But which longevity supplements actually have evidence behind them? And how do you separate the signal from the marketing noise? Here is what we know in 2026.

Why Supplementing for Longevity Makes Biological Sense

Aging is not a single process. It is the cumulative result of multiple interacting failures at the cellular level: declining NAD+ levels, accumulating DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, chronic inflammation, and more. The good news is that each of these pathways can, at least in principle, be supported or slowed with the right interventions.

Longevity supplements target these specific mechanisms rather than just masking symptoms. The goal is to support healthspan, which means the number of years you remain healthy, energetic, and functional, not just lifespan alone.

The Tier-One Longevity Supplements

These are compounds with the strongest combination of mechanistic evidence, human safety data, and real-world adoption among longevity researchers.

1. NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide) - The NAD+ Precursor

NMN sits at the top of almost every serious longevity supplement list because it addresses one of the most well-documented features of biological aging: the decline in NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) levels. By age 50, most people have roughly half the NAD+ they had at 20. Since NAD+ is essential for energy production, DNA repair, and sirtuin activation, this decline has wide-ranging consequences.

NMN is a direct precursor to NAD+. It is absorbed in the gut and converted to NAD+ in cells, raising levels meaningfully within weeks. A 2023 clinical trial published in GeroScience confirmed that 500mg daily of NMN raised blood NAD+ by over 38% in healthy adults aged 40 to 65, with no significant adverse effects.

What makes NMN particularly compelling as a longevity supplement is the breadth of downstream effects that come from restoring NAD+: improved mitochondrial function, better sleep quality, enhanced cognitive performance, and support for cellular repair mechanisms that slow with age.

2. Resveratrol -- The Sirtuin Activator

Resveratrol is a polyphenol found in red grape skins that activates SIRT1, one of the key sirtuin proteins involved in cellular repair and metabolic regulation. On its own, resveratrol's bioavailability is poor; it is rapidly metabolised in the gut. But taken alongside NMN, which supplies the NAD+ that sirtuins depend on, the combination creates a genuine synergistic effect.

This is why the NMN plus resveratrol pairing became famous through David Sinclair's public protocol. The mechanistic rationale is well-supported: resveratrol presses the accelerator on sirtuin activity while NMN ensures the fuel tank is full. For a full breakdown, see our NMN and resveratrol guide.

Best taken with fat to maximise absorption. Standard doses range from 250mg to 500mg daily.

3. Spermidine -- The Autophagy Trigger

Spermidine is a polyamine found naturally in wheat germ, mushrooms, and aged cheese. It is one of the most reliable known inducers of autophagy, the cellular recycling process by which damaged proteins and organelles are cleared away. Autophagy declines with age, and this decline is closely linked to the accumulation of cellular debris associated with neurodegenerative disease and accelerated aging.

Clinical trials in older adults have shown spermidine supplementation improves memory performance and reduces markers of inflammation. It stacks well with NMN because NMN supports energy production and DNA repair while spermidine handles cellular cleanup via autophagy. Typical doses range from 1mg to 5mg daily.

4. Quercetin -- The Senolytic

Quercetin is a flavonoid with a specific role in longevity supplementation: it is a senolytic, meaning it helps clear senescent cells. Senescent cells are those that have stopped dividing but refuse to die. They accumulate with age and secrete inflammatory signals (the "senescence-associated secretory phenotype" or SASP) that damage surrounding tissue and accelerate aging.

Quercetin, often taken in combination with the drug dasatinib in research settings, has shown the ability to selectively eliminate senescent cells in animal models, with some early human data now supporting this effect. As an over-the-counter supplement, it is most commonly used at 500mg to 1000mg on intermittent schedules (e.g. 2 to 3 days per month) rather than daily.

5. Magnesium Threonate -- For Brain Longevity

Most magnesium supplements do not cross the blood-brain barrier effectively. Magnesium threonate is a specific form shown in research to increase brain magnesium levels, supporting synaptic density and cognitive reserve. Since cognitive decline is one of the most significant threats to healthspan quality, supporting brain magnesium levels is increasingly viewed as a longevity strategy, not just a nootropic one.

The Longevity Supplement Hierarchy

Tier 1 (strongest evidence): NMN, Resveratrol
Tier 2 (good mechanistic support): Spermidine, Quercetin, Magnesium Threonate
Tier 3 (emerging / more evidence needed): Fisetin, Pterostilbene, Urolithin A

Emerging Longevity Supplements Worth Watching

These compounds have credible mechanisms and growing research interest, but the human evidence base is still thinner than Tier 1 compounds.

Fisetin

Fisetin is a flavonoid found in strawberries and apples. Like quercetin, it has senolytic properties. A Mayo Clinic study found fisetin reduced senescent cell burden in mice by up to 70%, with notable extensions in healthspan. Human trials are underway but not yet completed. Doses in trials range from 1000mg to 2000mg on intermittent protocols.

Urolithin A

Urolithin A is produced when gut bacteria metabolise ellagic acid from pomegranates and walnuts. It is one of the most potent known inducers of mitophagy, the process by which damaged mitochondria are cleared and replaced. Since mitochondrial dysfunction is central to age-related decline in energy and muscle strength, urolithin A has attracted significant research interest. Not everyone produces it from food (gut microbiome composition matters), so supplementing directly bypasses this variability.

Pterostilbene

A methylated analogue of resveratrol with substantially better bioavailability and a longer half-life in the body. Some researchers prefer it over resveratrol for this reason, though the overall evidence base is smaller. It crosses the blood-brain barrier more readily, which may make it particularly relevant for brain longevity applications.

How to Build a Longevity Supplement Stack

The most effective approach to longevity supplementation is layered: start with the best-evidenced compounds, establish a stable baseline, then add additional compounds once you understand your individual response.

A practical starting protocol looks like this:

  • Foundation: High-quality NMN (500mg daily, morning) to restore NAD+ levels. This is the single most impactful step for most people over 35.
  • Amplifier: Resveratrol (250mg to 500mg with fat, same time as NMN) to activate the sirtuin pathways that NMN fuels.
  • Cellular cleanup: Spermidine (2mg to 3mg daily) for autophagy support, or quercetin on an intermittent schedule for senolytic effects.
  • Brain support: Magnesium threonate in the evening to support cognitive reserve.

What About NMN Dosage for Longevity Specifically?

Most human research has used doses between 250mg and 1000mg of NMN daily. For longevity purposes specifically, the current consensus among researchers and practitioners tends to land around 500mg per day as the practical sweet spot: high enough to produce meaningful NAD+ elevation, low enough to remain cost-effective and avoid any potential tolerance issues.

Age matters too. Younger adults (under 40) may do well at 250mg, while those over 50 often benefit most from the 500mg to 750mg range, where NAD+ decline is most pronounced. For a detailed breakdown, see our NMN dosage guide.

The Lifestyle Foundation: Supplements Are a Multiplier, Not a Substitute

The most effective longevity supplements work best on a strong lifestyle foundation. The research consistently shows that sleep, exercise, diet, and stress management are the primary drivers of healthspan. Supplements amplify these effects; they do not replace them.

Key lifestyle factors that enhance the effectiveness of longevity supplements include:

  • Exercise: High-intensity interval training and resistance training both stimulate NAD+ production independently. NMN supplementation can compound these effects significantly. See our piece on NMN and exercise for details.
  • Fasting or time-restricted eating: Both activate autophagy through AMPK and mTOR pathways, complementing spermidine and quercetin supplementation.
  • Sleep: Deep sleep is when most cellular repair occurs. NMN's support for NAD+-dependent DNA repair mechanisms makes sleep quality directly relevant to its longevity effects.
  • Diet: A diet rich in polyphenols, omega-3 fatty acids, and low in ultra-processed foods reduces baseline inflammatory load, allowing longevity supplements to work more effectively.

Quality Matters: What to Look for in Longevity Supplements

With the longevity supplement market growing rapidly, quality control has become a genuine concern. Substandard products with inaccurate labelling, poor bioavailability, or contamination can be ineffective at best and harmful at worst. Before purchasing any supplement for longevity purposes, check for:

  • Third-party testing: Look for a certificate of analysis (CoA) from an independent laboratory confirming purity and potency.
  • GMP manufacturing: Good Manufacturing Practice certification ensures consistent production standards.
  • Clear ingredient sourcing: Know where the active compound comes from. For NMN specifically, look for beta-NMN (the biologically active form).
  • Transparent labelling: Avoid proprietary blends where individual doses are hidden.

Are Longevity Supplements Safe?

The compounds covered in this guide all have generally favourable safety profiles at standard doses, though individual circumstances vary. NMN has been studied in multiple human trials with no significant adverse effects reported at doses up to 1200mg daily. Resveratrol is well-tolerated up to about 1g, with GI discomfort occasionally reported at higher doses.

The most important safety consideration is drug interactions. Resveratrol inhibits certain liver enzymes (CYP450), which can affect how some medications are metabolised. If you take prescription drugs, always check with your GP before starting a longevity supplement protocol.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the single best supplement for longevity?
Based on current evidence, NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) has the strongest case as a longevity supplement for most adults over 35. It addresses one of the most well-documented features of biological aging -- declining NAD+ levels -- through a well-understood mechanism with confirmed human clinical trial support.

How long does it take for longevity supplements to work?
NMN typically produces measurable NAD+ increases within 2 to 4 weeks, with subjective energy improvements often reported in this window. Longevity benefits are cumulative and operate over months and years rather than days. Think of it as investing in cellular health, not an immediate treatment.

Can I take multiple longevity supplements together?
Yes, and many of them work synergistically. NMN and resveratrol are the most studied pairing. Spermidine and quercetin target different pathways (autophagy and senolytic activity respectively) and can be combined with NMN without interaction concerns at standard doses.

Are longevity supplements worth it if I already eat well and exercise?
A healthy lifestyle dramatically reduces the baseline deficit these supplements address. That said, NAD+ decline with age occurs regardless of lifestyle, and even very fit, healthy individuals over 40 typically have significantly lower NAD+ than they did at 25. Supplementing to restore NAD+ is additive to lifestyle, not a substitute for it.

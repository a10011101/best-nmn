The Core Sinclair Protocol (2026 Edition)

1

NMN: 750mg Daily

Sinclair takes 750mg of NMN every morning, typically mixed into yoghurt. For comparison, most clinical studies use 500mg daily — see our NMN dosage guide for recommended amounts based on age and health goals. This is higher than the 500mg used in most clinical trials, reflecting his personal optimisation based on blood NAD+ measurements. He's stated that 500mg is sufficient for most people, but he personally prefers 750mg for maximal effect. Importantly, he uses pure beta-NMN (not the cheaper alpha form) and emphasises third-party testing for purity.

2

Resveratrol: 1g Daily

The famous "Sinclair stack" pairs NMN with 1 gram of resveratrol. Resveratrol activates sirtuin proteins (SIRT1 in particular), which are NAD+-dependent enzymes involved in DNA repair and cellular stress resistance. Sinclair takes resveratrol with a fatty meal (like yoghurt) to improve absorption. He prefers trans-resveratrol from Japanese knotweed extract, which has better bioavailability than grape-derived versions.

3

Morning Timing with Food

Sinclair takes both NMN and resveratrol in the morning with breakfast. This aligns with the body's natural circadian NAD+ rhythm, which peaks in the morning. Taking NAD+ precursors in the evening can disrupt sleep by interfering with the natural NAD+ decline that occurs at night. The fatty yoghurt also helps with resveratrol absorption.

4

Metformin: 500–850mg (Optional)

Sinclair has also taken metformin (a diabetes drug with potential longevity benefits) at 500–850mg daily, though he's been more cautious about this recently due to potential muscle side effects. In 2025 interviews, he indicated he cycles metformin rather than taking it continuously. This is not part of his core NMN/resveratrol stack but is worth mentioning as part of his broader longevity regimen.

Why This Combination Works: The Science

The NAD+ → Sirtuin Pathway

NMN raises NAD+ levels. Resveratrol activates sirtuins. Together, they create a synergistic effect: more NAD+ provides the fuel, while resveratrol increases the activity of the enzymes (sirtuins) that use that fuel for DNA repair, mitochondrial biogenesis, and cellular stress resistance.

Human Clinical Evidence

While no single trial has tested the exact Sinclair stack in humans, the components have strong individual support:

  • NMN: Multiple human trials show 500–1000mg/day increases blood NAD+ by 40–60% within 2–4 weeks, with improvements in insulin sensitivity, muscle function, and energy.
  • Resveratrol: Human studies show benefits for cardiovascular health, glucose metabolism, and inflammation markers at doses of 500–1000mg/day.
  • Combination: Animal studies show NMN + resveratrol together produce greater benefits than either alone in measures of mitochondrial function and lifespan extension.

How Sinclair Measures Results

One thing that sets Sinclair apart from most supplement advocates is that he actually measures his NAD+ levels. He uses regular blood tests to track:

  • Blood NAD+ concentration — his baseline and post-NMN levels (he's reported 40–60% increases)
  • Biological age tests — using epigenetic clocks (DNA methylation markers) to assess cellular age vs chronological age
  • Inflammatory markers — CRP, IL-6, and other biomarkers of systemic inflammation
  • Metabolic markers — fasting glucose, insulin sensitivity, lipid panel

He's reported in multiple interviews that his biological age (as measured by epigenetic clocks) tests significantly younger than his chronological age — though he's cautious about attributing this solely to supplements, noting that diet, exercise, sleep, and stress management all contribute.

What Changed in Sinclair's Protocol for 2026

Sinclair's protocol has evolved based on emerging research. Notable updates as of 2026:

  • Higher NMN dose: He moved from 500mg to 750mg after tracking his blood NAD+ response more carefully
  • More cautious on metformin: Following data showing metformin may blunt exercise-induced muscle protein synthesis, he's moved to cycling it rather than daily dosing
  • Added quercetin: Increased focus on senolytics — compounds that clear aged, dysfunctional cells from the body
  • Reduced alcohol to near zero: Alcohol is a known NAD+ consumer; eliminating it preserves the gains from NMN supplementation
  • Cold exposure added: Regular cold showers or ice baths to activate additional stress-response pathways that complement NAD+/sirtuin activation

Sinclair's Perspective on Supplement Quality

Sinclair has repeatedly stressed that NMN purity matters enormously. The NMN supplement market is poorly regulated, and independent testing has found that many products contain significantly less NMN than labelled — or contain impurities from low-grade synthesis.

Key quality markers he recommends checking:

  • Beta-NMN form — not alpha-NMN, which is less stable and less bioavailable
  • Third-party purity testing — independent lab verification (not self-reported)
  • GMP certification — manufacturing quality standard
  • No unnecessary excipients — fillers like magnesium stearate dilute the active ingredient

What is NMN and Why Are Canadians Taking It?

Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN) is a direct precursor to NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide), the coenzyme that powers mitochondrial energy production, DNA repair and the activation of sirtuin longevity proteins. NAD+ declines by approximately 50 percent between your 20s and 50s, a well-documented biochemical change linked to reduced energy, slower recovery, impaired cognition and metabolic changes in aging adults.

Human clinical trials confirm that oral NMN raises blood NAD+ levels measurably and delivers functional improvements at doses of 250mg to 500mg per day. Canadian adults looking for evidence-based longevity supplementation have embraced NMN, particularly following coverage in mainstream media outlets and interest from the Canadian longevity research community. NMN is widely taken alongside resveratrol and TMG in the Sinclair protocol, which has driven the majority of North American awareness of the supplement category.

Health Canada NNHPD Status for NMN

In Canada, natural health products are regulated under the Natural Health Products Regulations (SOR/2003-196), administered by Health Canada's Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate (NNHPD). To be commercially marketed in Canada with health claims, a natural health product must hold a Natural Product Number (NPN), issued following Health Canada review of safety, efficacy and quality evidence. NPN-authorized products may display the NPN on their label.

Several NMN products have obtained NPN authorization in Canada, meaning they have passed Health Canada's review and can be lawfully sold with approved health claims in Canadian retail. However, most of the major international NMN brands selling to Canadian consumers online do not hold Canadian NPN authorization and do not make Health Canada-approved claims in their Canadian-facing marketing. They sell under the personal importation policy rather than as Canadian-authorized natural health products.

Health Canada's personal importation policy permits Canadian residents to import up to a 90-day supply of a natural health product for personal use from abroad. This is the legal basis on which the vast majority of Canadian NMN purchases from US and UK brands operate. The policy is well-established, routinely enforced at the practical level as permissive for personal-use quantities of non-scheduled supplement products, and poses no meaningful legal risk to Canadian buyers purchasing reputable NMN brands for personal use.

Customs and Duty Tips for Canadian NMN Buyers

Importing NMN from the United States to Canada benefits from the CUSMA (Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement) trade framework, which eliminates customs duty on qualifying natural health products including most dietary supplements. The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) applies the applicable tariff code, typically nil duty for NMN capsules under US origin rules. HST or GST and PST will apply in most provinces to goods above the CAD 20 de minimis threshold for postal or courier imports.

Practical import steps for Canadian buyers:

  • Orders from the US below approximately CAD 20: typically cleared without charges.
  • Orders from the US above CAD 20: subject to provincial HST/GST and potentially a brokerage fee from Canada Post or FedEx. Canada Post typically charges a CAD 9.95 flat handling fee; FedEx and UPS fees vary and can be higher for brokered courier shipments.
  • Orders from the UK (non-CUSMA): standard MFN duty rate applies, but NMN supplements typically fall under a zero or low duty rate. UK VAT is not charged to Canadian buyers. UK brands generally collect and remit no Canadian taxes at checkout; taxes and any brokerage fees are assessed at the border by CBSA.
  • To minimise brokerage fees: choose brands that use Canada Post (Lettermail or Tracked Packet International) rather than FedEx or UPS, as Canada Post brokerage fees are lower for small packages.
